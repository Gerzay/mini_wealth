📊 Budgeting basics
Track what comes in and goes out, even the small stuff — awareness alone changes behavior.
Know your fixed costs first. What's left is what you actually get to decide about.
Give every euro a job: needs, wants, or savings — nothing just floats.
✂️ Cutting costs
Cook more at home and plan meals before you shop — most food waste is an unplanned-meal problem.
Compare prices and go store-brand where the difference genuinely doesn't matter to you.
Cancel subscriptions you're not actually using — check your list once a quarter.
Wait 24 hours before a non-essential purchase. Most impulse wants fade; the real ones don't.
Bundle errands into one trip — fewer trips out means fewer chances to spend on impulse.
🐷 Saving habits
Pay yourself first — move money to savings the moment you're paid, not whatever's left over.
Automate what you can. Saving shouldn't depend on remembering or willpower.
Aim to build 3–6 months of expenses as a cushion — it turns emergencies into inconveniences.
🧠 Money mindset
Guilt about spending is usually about misalignment with what you value, not the money itself.
A plan turns "should I have bought that?" into "this was already accounted for."
Money is a tool for the life you want — not a scoreboard to win.
A quick tour of what each tab does and how the small interactions work.
🏡 Home
My budget is your own plan as a pie \u2014 what you have decided goes to Needs, Wants and Savings & Debt each month, with the euros and the share next to each one, and how far that share sits from the 50/20/30 target. It replaces the textbook diagram that used to sit here, which showed the rule rather than your plan. Underneath it, Your spending vs the rule is the other half of the picture: not what you intended, but where the money actually went.
The three tiles are one sum you can read across: What I own (every account added up) minus What I owe (every loan) is Net worth. Your bank balance is deliberately not part of it — that is the big card at the top, and it answers a different question. Tap the Net worth tile to see the sum line by line, account by account, so the figure is never something you have to take on trust.
Your balance for the current calendar month, plus an all-time total underneath it. Below that, the 50/20/30 pie shows how your actual spending splits across Needs, Wants, Savings, and Debt against those targets — and your to-do list, each item optionally dated.
💳 Transactions
Add income or an expense with the toggle, and pick a date if you're logging something from earlier. Search or filter by category above the list. Tap the colored dot on any entry to reclassify it (Needs → Wants → Savings → Debt → Balance / transfer), edit the amount directly, or tap × then ✓ to delete it.
⚖️ Balance is the third button, next to Income and Expense, and it exists so you never have to work out a correction yourself. Tap it, type the number your bank app is showing you right now — with a minus in front if you're overdrawn — and the line underneath tells you in plain words what it is about to do before you tap Add: what WealthOS thinks you have, what your bank says, and the entry it will create to make the two agree. The description box is just an optional note for which account it was. Use it when you carry an overdraft over from last month, when you move money between your own accounts, or any time the app has drifted from your bank. These entries are grey and count as neither income nor spending, so they correct your balance without touching the 50/20/30 pie or any budget line.
🧾 Bills
A bill tagged Debt gets a Pays off picker. Point it at a loan and one tap logs the expense and takes the same amount off what you owe, so the payment is recorded once rather than in two places. Without it a loan payment that exists both as a bill and as a loan payment gets logged twice \u2014 the money leaves your bank twice and only one of the two lowers the balance. The Loans page watches for that: if it spots a bill that looks like it pays a loan, it says so in red and offers Join them up, which links the two and removes the loan's own Log button so there is only ever one place to tap.
A bill that is really a transfer — money you move into your own savings rather than spend — gets a Lands in picker (it appears on any bill tagged Savings, and on any bill already pointed at an account). Pick the account and one tap does both halves: the expense goes into Transactions and the same amount is credited to that account, dated today. Undo last takes back both. Without this the money left your bank and arrived nowhere, which is what makes a savings balance drift away from reality month after month.
The small day box next to each amount is the day of the month the money leaves your account. Fill it in and two things happen: the Bills page sorts itself by what's coming next instead of the order you typed them, and Home grows a ⏰ Due in the next 7 days list with the total. A bill you've already logged this month drops off that list, so it only ever shows what still needs doing.
Your recurring monthly expenses, pre-loaded once. Tap "Add to transactions" to log one instantly instead of retyping it. Add your own with the form up top, tap a dot to change its category, or × then ✓ to remove one.
🎯 Projects
The three tiles at the top: Set aside is what you have actually put towards your goals so far, added up across all of them — either what you typed into a goal's Add box, or, for a goal with a source, what its account or budget line really holds. The second tile is the opposite end: everything you are aiming for added together. Needed per month only counts goals that have a target date — it is what those goals demand between them, each month, from now until their dates. A goal with no date asks nothing of you, so it stays out of that figure, and the line under the tiles says how many are being ignored for that reason.
Every goal has a Fed by box. Leave it on "I enter it myself" and it works as before, with an Add box. Point it at a savings account or a budget line instead and the goal reads its own progress — no typing — plus the month it finishes at the rate money is actually going in. If the account or line it points at is later renamed or deleted, the goal says so in red rather than silently freezing at an old number.
Things you are putting money toward — a trip, a replacement laptop, a cushion. Name one, set a target, and add a target date if there is a deadline: it then works out what you need to put aside each month to get there, and the tiles at the top total that across every project. A project is an earmark, not a real pot — the money still sits in your accounts, so projects are deliberately left out of net worth to avoid counting it twice. Tap the name, the target or the date to change them; × removes a project.
📋 Budget
My rule at the top of the Budget page sets the shares everything else is measured against. 50/20/30 is a starting point written for a different housing market, not a law — if your rent makes 50% impossible, a target you can never hit tells you nothing. Type your own three percentages, or take one of the presets: 50/30/20 for more room to live, 60/10/30 for high rent while still saving, 70/10/20 when rent is heavy for a while. Use my own plan’s shares turns whatever you have actually planned into the target, which is the honest option when the plan already reflects your real constraints. The page warns you if the three do not add up to 100%, but still uses them. Both pies on Home follow this rule too, so the whole app judges you by your own yardstick.
Each of the three groups carries its own icon and a short note saying what belongs in it — 🏠 Needs is what you would still have to pay if you cancelled everything else this month, 🎬 Wants is what you choose, 🌱 Savings & Debt is money still yours afterwards or debt you are clearing. Each note lists the usual lines and the one trap to avoid for that group. Every budget line also gets a small picture of its own, matched on its name — 🏠 Loyer, 🛒 Courses, ⛽ Essence, 🚇 Navigo — so you can find a line by eye instead of reading. A name it does not recognise simply takes its group icon.
When you log a bill that no budget line claims, a line for it is created so the money shows up in your plan. That is useful, but it can quietly raise a group total when your plan already covered the same money under a different name — your €230 "SG" line and a €100 "Assurance vie Société Générale" bill, for instance. Those lines are now marked: a note at the top of the page says how much of the plan arrived that way and names the lines, and each one carries a ↳ note of its own. Delete any your plan already covers and the group total drops back.
↺ Restore my starting plan at the bottom of the Budget page puts your written plan back — all 20 lines and the €2,400 income — however the budget got emptied. It asks first if you already have lines, because it replaces them. Your transactions are never touched. Above it, the small Add a budget line form takes a name, a group and a monthly amount, for anything that isn't one of your bills.
Your plan for the month, next to what actually happened. Enter your monthly income and the 50/20/30 split fills in as a starting plan; the line at the top shows what is still unplanned (or over-planned). Each group fills up as you log expenses dated in the current month and turns red once you pass the plan.
Inside a group the budget runs line by line — Loyer, Courses, Navigo. Lines come from your bills: tapping "Add to transactions" on a bill gives it its own line the first time, and "Fill lines from my bills" does the whole list at once. Neither ever creates a duplicate — a bill is skipped when a line already covers it, so an "Internet" line absorbs "Internet Bouygues". A line claims an expense when the description contains the line's name and the transaction already sits in that group, so "Courses" catches both "Courses Carrefour" and "Courses Lidl". Whatever no line claims shows as "Other", which is your unbudgeted spending. Once a group has lines its plan becomes their sum, the small dropdown under a line moves it to another group, and × removes it.
🏦 Loans
Record payments to bring the balance down, or set an annual rate to see monthly interest (principal × rate ÷ 12) — applying it logs an editable entry. Add other loans or rename/delete as needed.
📊 Dashboard plan vs the minimum sits at the top of the Loans page whenever your Dashboard has loan figures in it. The two pages answer different questions and were quietly disagreeing: the Dashboard shows your plan with extra repayments, this page shows what happens if you only ever pay the minimum. The panel puts both dates side by side and says what the minimum costs you in years. It also points out when the two start from different balances, so you know which one to update.
Set the monthly payment on every loan — it's the field under the balance. That one number is what turns the page from a list of balances into an answer: the card then tells you how many payments are left and the month it clears, and the tile at the top gives you the total owed plus the date you're debt free (the slowest loan sets that date). With an interest rate set, the count uses real amortisation, not a division, and shows the interest still to come. If a payment is too small to cover the month's interest, it says so in red instead of inventing a date — that balance is growing, not shrinking.
Once a monthly payment is set, the Log button appears on any loan, not just the splits: one tap writes the expense into Transactions and takes it off the balance, with the same "already logged this month" badge and Undo last.
Paiement en plusieurs fois (3x, 4x, 10x) goes in the second form. Give it the total, how many times, Needs or Wants, the fees (0 if sans frais) and the month of the first payment — that last box is what fixes the end date: a 3x whose first payment was in September finishes in November, whatever today's date is. If the plan started before this month, the payments already behind you are counted automatically, so a 4x begun in September opens owing €300 of €600 rather than the full amount, and the preview spells that out before you tap Set up. A plan whose last payment has already passed isn't added at all — there'd be nothing to track. The card then shows how many are paid of the total, the month it started, and the month it clears; if the end date goes by with money still owing, it says so in red instead of quietly moving the date. Each month, one tap on Log €x does both halves at once: it writes the expense into Transactions in the category you chose, and takes that much off what you owe. The card tells you how many payments are left and whether you've already logged this month, and Undo last fixes a double tap. Don't log the whole purchase as one expense in the month you buy — the money hasn't left yet, and it would wreck that month's budget.
📁 Accounts
Renaming is safe. When you rename an account, a loan or a budget line, everything pointing at it follows — the bill that pays into it, the goal fed by it, and the past transactions that recorded it. Nothing is left pointing at a name that no longer exists.
Accounts come in two shapes, and the choice matters. A savings account keeps every deposit with its date, counts towards your Savings total, can receive a monthly transfer from Bills and can feed a goal. An investment account is a single figure you overwrite when the value changes. The box next to the name when you add one lets you pick; an account that is already the wrong shape has a Track this as a savings account button on its card, and whatever value it holds becomes its opening deposit. A Livret A, LDDS, LEP, Livret Jeune, PEL or CEL also picks up its official ceiling automatically.
If you put the same amount into a savings account every month, type it into Put in every month € and tap Make it a bill. That creates a "Virement <account>" bill already pointed at the account, so from then on one tap a month logs the expense and credits the account together. The card then tells you whether this month's transfer has gone in yet.
Every account you hold, Livret A and LDDS included. For a plain account (PEA, assurance-vie, PER…) edit the balance directly or use "Add amount" to adjust it by a delta. Livret A and LDDS work slightly differently: their balance is built from dated deposits, so you change it with "Add amount" rather than by typing over it, and they carry an annual rate — the projection uses the real quinzaine method (interest per fortnight), and applying it logs an editable entry. Rename or delete any account by tapping its name or the ×. Export a readable HTML summary from the button at the bottom, save or load a backup file, and — at the very bottom — reset the app, which erases everything on this device and puts it back to a fresh start. It asks twice, and a backup is the only way back.
📊 History
Net worth, an account trend chart, and the last 3 months broken down by category — always 3 months, even if some are empty.
🗂️ Dashboard
The first row is the point de départ, and every month below it is projected from that row — so if the starting figures are stale, the whole table is. The panel above it checks them against the rest of the app: your Livret A against the real account balance, the bank line against your transactions, the loan against what you still owe. When one disagrees it shows both numbers and a button to adopt the real one, and the entire column shifts to match. When they all agree it says so in green. If the app side is empty — a savings account with nothing logged into it — it offers no button at all, because a zero there means "nothing recorded", not "nothing in the account"; it tells you to put the real amount into the account first. Every change you do apply leaves an Undo next to it. The quickest route of all is the box at the bottom of that panel: type what the account really holds today and tap Set both — it writes a dated movement into the account to bring it to that figure and sets the starting row, so the two can't drift apart. Undo takes back both halves.
Your LBP payoff and savings allocation plan. The first row is your fixed starting point; every row after that recalculates Solde LBP, Livret A, and Capital prêt automatically once you fill in vers LBP / vers Livret A / vers Prêt. Check a month off once it's done — the summary cards at the top follow your last checked month, not the far-future projection. Add rows to extend the plan.

🍃 Simple mode
The switch at the top of Home decides how much of the app you look at. Simple keeps four tabs in the menu — Home, Transactions, Bills and Projects — and puts the other eight on a row of buttons on Home, one tap away. Everything shows all twelve in the menu as before. Nothing is ever hidden from you and nothing is deleted; it only changes what the menu holds, and the app remembers which mode you left it in. If you are sitting on one of the tucked-away tabs when you switch to Simple, it takes you back to Home rather than leaving you on a page the menu no longer shows.
Next to that switch is Text size, four steps from small to largest. It scales the whole app — text, buttons, charts, tables — not just the writing, so nothing ends up with big type in a small box. The app opens one step up from its old size, and your choice is remembered. Every size has been checked on a phone-width screen and on a tablet, including the dashboard table, so nothing runs off the side at any of them.
Simple mode also puts two short panels on Home: What to do, and when (most days nothing, once a week tap your bills, once a month check your balances) and How one euro travels, which is the short answer to how the tabs relate: you tap a bill, it becomes a transaction, it fills in a budget line, a transfer lands in an account, and that account can feed a goal. The practical consequence is that you mostly tap bills and the rest fills itself in.
🔑 A few things that apply everywhere
Balance / transfer is the fifth dot colour (grey) and it is not spending. Use it for money that moves without being spent: an overdraft you are carrying over from last month, a transfer between two of your own accounts, a correction to make the app match your bank. It counts toward your balance, but it stays out of the 50/20/30 pie and out of every budget line, so one old overdraft doesn't make this month look like a disaster. Bank fees are real spending — leave those as Needs.
Deleting always asks twice — tap × once, then ✓ to confirm or ✕ to cancel. Nothing deletes on the first tap.
Names are editable pretty much everywhere — tap a bill, account, savings, or loan name to rename it in place.
Everything is saved automatically as you go — no save button anywhere.
